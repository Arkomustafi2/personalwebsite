Journey & Mistakes of My Undergrad Life

  • Home
  • ← Blog

Life becomes uncertain when you dream of something and eventually achieve it. If you are a person with only a few dreams and you achieve them, you will find yourself off the track — because life is a long journey and we have to plan big always. If you don't have a plan for the whole road ahead, somewhere along the way you will get stuck, just like I did. So plan before it is too late, and work accordingly.

When I got admitted to Dhaka University I was living in my dreams. Sometimes it felt unreal. I had no mentor to guide me through the harsh reality waiting in front of me. In the first semester, as a first-year student, I skipped study — explored the new city, gossiped, stayed curious. Then the pandemic came. We were in lockdown for a long time. I had no laptop then, and I was alone with my mobile phone, passing each day at home. This created a long gap between myself and study.

At that time I was getting a scholarship from the Alumni Association of the EEE department. With this money and some savings from my mother, I bought my first laptop. After that, I was too excited about technology and learned whatever I could find. Coding, OS setup, tooling — but the gap between study and myself only grew wider. Another reason was that when I came to study engineering, I had assumed less memorization and more practical work. The online lectures and theory-heavy syllabi were draining me.

When most people were vaccinated and the virus was slowing down, the department called for the first-semester exam — which we had actually completed six months earlier. So while we had just finished the second semester's coursework, we had to go back and give first-semester exams, due to DU's administrative rules. After that, we got barely a day before the second-semester exams in Curzon Hall. At that point, I had no room in Dhaka. I had to find a mess to live in — there were no non-political seats in the hall.

Adjusting to Dhaka's environment was hard. The food was a major problem — hotels were expensive and not always clean, and the mess had no meals. I had to walk nearly 1.5 km to Jagannath Hall for two meals a day. I also carried water from campus because the mess water was undrinkable. Coming from a middle-class family, I had a fixed budget and never pressurized my family to send more.

Since the first year was mostly online, I started making proper friends only after the second year began. Not being a fan of traditional theory-based academics, I invested time in volunteering.

I joined the Dhaka University Rover Scout Group — the only member from the engineering faculty. I enjoyed working with people from different departments. The most exciting part was the duty on 21st February, which every volunteer loved. The second avenue was IEEE. I joined as a member and eventually became Treasurer of the IEEE Student Branch at DU. Through IEEE, I explored Collabratec, found the IEEE Puzzlers group, started solving their puzzles, and eventually made some of my own. Met some genuinely remarkable people through that community.

The volunteering life was good. The academic life was a fight. Both were real.
