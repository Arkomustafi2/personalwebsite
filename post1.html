Academic Depression

  • Home
  • ← Blog

I am a simple student who came to the University of Dhaka from a family where no one else had completed their education from any public or private university. When I was in class 10, I dreamed big. I dreamed to get admission into a good college so I could go to a good university. Obviously, I was not fully aware of what university or which subject then. I got board scholarships in all my board exams.

After the admission test, I got admitted into the University of Dhaka — a dream university for everyone. But things started to change very rapidly, and I was not able to cope. Dhaka is totally different from Rajshahi. I tried to do my best to adapt to this city and to a completely new English curriculum.

In the meantime, Covid-19 spread all over the world and there was a huge gap in study. Though I used that time for skill development, there was no impact of it in my academics. I learned web development, some basic security concepts, how to set up a PC, and many other things. I don't personally like the teaching style and system in DU. If you don't read, you will get bad grades — but if you get a bad CGPA in DU EEE, you will continuously keep getting bad grades. In the first two years, I was barely serious in my studies. I liked reading books, meeting new friends, engaging in different volunteering activities, and I was also in a relationship.

From class five through intermediate, I had never failed to get a board scholarship. So, I had never experienced academic failure before. But in university, I started getting bad grades from the second semester. After every semester I tried to find my fault and fix it, but for some reason, I was not able to cut a good grade. The pressure was immense — to prove myself — but I failed again and again.

In the darkest days of life, you find out who will be there for you unconditionally. She left me when I needed her the most. That too became another thing to overcome, another reason to fight back to my seat.

In the last semester, I tried my best to do well in exams. Surprisingly, I still got bad grades. In two courses where I thought I'd get a 4, I got only a 3. In another two, even worse — despite giving what felt like good exams. So there is no one to blame. I think God's plan is the best plan.
