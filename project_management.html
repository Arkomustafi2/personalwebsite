What is this course really about?

Project Management covers how organizations plan, execute, and close projects on time, within budget, and with expected quality. It applies structured knowledge areas (PMBOK) — from scope and scheduling through HR and risk — to real-world problems. The course emphasizes analytical tools like PERT, Earned Value Management (EVM), cost estimation methods, and organizational structures that determine how much authority a project manager holds. Mastery requires connecting theory (concepts) to quantitative problem-solving (formulas and worked examples).

🏗️ Project Management Fundamentals (Class 1 — 8 Mar 2026)

What is a Project?

A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.

Examples:
• Making a mobile app
• Building a bridge
• Software update
Key Traits:
• Has a start and end date
• Produces something new or different
• Not routine work
A project differs from ongoing operations. Operations are continuous; projects are temporary and unique.

Project and Competitive Advantage

Organizations undertake projects to create competitive advantage. Regular operations do not lead to competitive advantage — without it, a firm cannot survive in the market.

Examples: Annual design update of phones, software feature releases, new product launches.

5 Characteristics of a Project

1
Temporary — project does not last forever; it has a defined end.
2
Unique Deliverable — produces something new (product, service, or result).
3
Progressive Elaboration — developed step by step as understanding grows.
4
Resource Constraints — limited by time, cost, people, and material (the main parameters).
5
Purpose Driven — every project has a clear, defined purpose.

PMBOK — 9 Knowledge Areas

The PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge) is a standard guide for project management. It includes 9 knowledge areas. Areas 2–9 run parallel throughout the project; Integration Management (1) controls and connects everything.
#Knowledge AreaPurpose
1Project Integration ManagementCombines all parts of the project. Acts like a controller. Ensures everything works together.
2Project Scope ManagementDefines what work will be done. Prevents extra/unwanted work.
3Project Time ManagementScheduling tasks; ensuring project finishes on time.
4Project Cost ManagementBudget planning; controlling expenses.
5Project Quality ManagementEnsures output is good and standard.
6Project HR ManagementManaging team members; assigning roles and responsibilities.
7Project Communications ManagementSharing information properly; keeps everyone updated.
8Project Risk ManagementIdentifying problems before they happen; making backup plans.
9Project Procurement ManagementBuying goods/services from outside the organization.
Areas 2–9 must run parallel throughout the project. Scope, time, cost, quality, HR, communication, risk, procurement all happen together. This is why Integration Management (#1) controls and connects everything.

🏢 Organization, Structures & HR Management (Classes 2 & 3)

What is an Organization?

An organization is a group of people working together in a coordinated way to achieve common goals. It is not a building — it is the people and their coordinated activities. Every project needs to be done within an organization because it requires planning, coordination, and teamwork.

Organizational Boundary: The limit that separates an organization from its external environment (customers, suppliers, government, society).

Example: A factory interacts with → Customers (selling products) → Government (regulations) → Society (employment, environment impact)
An organization is an open system — it constantly interacts with its environment. It takes inputs (people, materials, information), processes them, and produces outputs (products, services), while adapting to feedback.

Types of Organizational Boundaries

Horizontal Boundary: Separates different departments or units at the same level (e.g., Marketing, Finance, HR). Focus: coordination and collaboration across functions.

Breaking horizontal boundaries → cross-functional teamwork.
Vertical Boundary: Separates levels of hierarchy — CEO → Manager → Employee. Defines authority and reporting relationships.

Reducing vertical boundaries → flatter structure, faster communication and decision-making.

4 Organizational Frames (Ways to View Organizations)

1. Structural Frame
Focuses on roles, responsibilities, coordinations, and control. Organizational charts define this frame.
Ex: A company defining job descriptions clearly.
2. Political Frame (Office Politics)
Organizations are coalitions of individuals and interest groups. Conflict of power is a key issue.
Ex: Departments competing for budget.
3. Human Resource Frame
Focuses on harmony between needs of the organization and needs of the people.
Ex: Employee training programs, work-life balance policies.
4. Symbolic Frame
Focuses on symbols and meanings related to events. Culture is important.
Ex: Company rituals, slogans, branding. Apple Inc. emphasizing innovation culture.

Project Human Resource Management (Class 3 — 6 Apr 2026)

Resource = valuable asset. Project HR management means managing the people involved in a project — team members, project managers, engineers, workers, staff.

ILO (International Labour Organization): Founded 1919, later became part of UN. Protects workers' rights, promotes fair working conditions, works for social justice.

Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 controls: workers' rights, working conditions, employer responsibilities.

Types of Jobs (Collar Jobs)

TypeDescriptionExamples
Blue CollarPhysical, skilled or semi-skilled laborFactory workers, electricians, drivers
White CollarOffice or professional/mental workTeachers, engineers, bankers
Brown CollarWork related to environment/sustainabilitySolar panel technicians, waste management workers

Types of Employees

Exempt Employees: Do NOT get overtime pay. Usually high-level or professional jobs.
Ex: Managers, engineers, company executives.
Non-exempt Employees: GET overtime pay. Usually hourly or lower-level jobs.
Ex: Factory workers, clerks, technicians.

Salary Issues

Salary Compression: New employees get almost the same salary as experienced employees.
Problem → Senior workers feel unfair treatment; motivation decreases.
Salary Inversion: New employees get MORE salary than older employees.
Problem → Creates anger and dissatisfaction.

Sources of Dissatisfaction

Things that make workers unhappy: Low salary, poor working conditions, no job security, bad management, lack of recognition.

Salary Remuneration = Total payment given to employees. Includes: Basic salary + bonus + allowances.
Perks = Extra benefits besides salary (free lunch, company car, health insurance, paid vacation). Purpose: increase motivation, attract employees.

6 Core/General Functions of HR

1
Recruitment & Selection: Finding and hiring the right people. HR identifies vacancies → advertises → collects applicants → selects through tests and interviews.
2
Training & Development: Improving employees' skills. Training = short-term, job-specific. Development = long-term, prepares for future roles. Increases productivity, efficiency, career growth.
3
Performance Management: Evaluating and improving employee performance. HR sets standards, monitors work, gives feedback, conducts appraisals.
4
Compensation & Benefits: Paying employees and providing rewards. Salary + wages + bonuses + health insurance + leave + perks. Ensures fair and competitive pay.
5
Employee Relations: Maintaining healthy relationship between employees and management. Handles conflicts, grievances, and communication. Good relations create positive work environment and increase job satisfaction.
6
Compliance & Legal Issues: Ensures organization follows labor laws and regulations (wages, working hours, safety, employee rights). Helps avoid legal problems and ensures ethical practices.

Project Communications Management (Class 4 — 22 Apr 2026)

Projects fail very often because of poor communication — not because of technical problems.

Good communication helps to: keep stakeholders informed, reduce misunderstandings and conflicts, improve decision making, coordinate team activities, track project progress.

Communication Management as a Process (3 Steps)

1
Plan Communication Management: Determines how communication will happen. Who needs information? What information? When? How will it be delivered?
2
Manage Communications: Creating and distributing information. Activities: sending reports, conducting meetings, sharing project updates, responding to stakeholder questions. Tools: Email, Meetings, Phone/Video call, Dashboard.
3
Monitor Communications: Ensures communication is working effectively. Checks: Are stakeholders receiving the information? Are there misunderstandings? Is communication clear?

3 Types of Communication Methods

PushInformation sent to specific people (one direction)Email, reports, letters
PullInformation stored somewhere; people access it when neededWebsite, knowledge base, project portal

Project Cost Management (Class 5 — 13 Apr 2026)

Focuses on planning, estimating, budgeting, and controlling costs so that the project can be completed within the approved budget.

Objectives: (1) Ensure project is completed within approved budget. (2) Control cost overruns. (3) Optimize resource utilization.

4 Main Processes of Project Cost Management

1
Cost Planning: Defines how project cost will be estimated.
2
Cost Estimation: Predicts the cost of resources needed for the project.
Methods: Analogous (based on past project), Parametric (statistical models), Bottom-up (detailed activity level), Three-point estimation.
3
Cost Budgeting: Aggregates estimated cost to establish total project budget. Includes: Cost baseline + Contingency reserve (PM's reserve for known unknowns) + Management reserve (Higher authority's reserve for unknown unknowns).
Project Budget = Cost Estimate + Contingency Reserve (PM) + Management Reserve (HA) Cost Baseline = determined by Project Manager
4
Cost Control: Monitors project spending and manages changes to the budget. Uses Earned Value Management (EVM).

Three-Point Estimation Formula

Weighted Average (Expected Cost), E = (O + 4M + P) / 6 Where: O = Optimistic estimate M = Most Likely estimate P = Pessimistic estimate

Earned Value Management (EVM) — Critical for Exam

EVM formulas are heavily tested. Know all 5 metrics and what the results mean.
TermSymbolMeaning
Planned ValuePVHow much work was supposed to be done (by value)
Earned ValueEVHow much work was actually completed (by value)
Actual CostACHow much was actually spent
Cost Variance CV = EV – AC (negative = over budget) Schedule Variance SV = EV – PV (negative = behind schedule) Cost Performance Index CPI = EV / AC (< 1 = over budget; > 1 = under budget) Schedule Performance Index SPI = EV / PV (< 1 = behind schedule; > 1 = ahead)
Example from notes: EV = $40,000 | PV = $50,000 | AC = $95,000

CV = 40,000 – 95,000 = –$55,000 → Over budget (budget overrun)
SV = 40,000 – 50,000 = –$10,000 → Behind schedule
CPI = 40,000 / 95,000 = 0.42 → Over budget
SPI = 40,000 / 50,000 = 0.80 → Behind schedule

Project Time Management (Class 6 — 19 Apr 2026)

Focuses on planning, scheduling, and controlling how time is used so a project is completed on schedule.

The three constraints of a project: Scope, Time, Budget.

5 Key Processes of Time Management

1
Activity Definition: Break the project into smaller tasks (activities). Ex: Build a website → Design, Coding, Testing, Deployment.
2
Activity Sequencing: Decide the order of tasks. Some must happen first; some can run parallel. Tools: Network diagnosis, Dependency mapping.
3
Activity Duration Estimation: Estimate how long each task will take. Methods: Expert judgement, Analogous estimation, Three-point estimation (Optimistic, Most likely, Pessimistic).
4
Schedule Development: Create the actual timeline. Tools: Gantt chart, Critical Path Method (CPM), Program Evaluation Review Technique (PERT).
5
Schedule Control: Track progress and adjust when needed. Compare planned vs actual value → Handle delays → Update schedule.

PERT — Program Evaluation Review Technique

PERT is a network-based project scheduling method that helps managers estimate the minimum time needed to complete a project by analyzing the time and dependencies of each activity.
Expected Time (TE) = (O + 4M + P) / 6 O = Optimistic time M = Most Likely time P = Pessimistic time

Project Scope Management (Class 7 — 20 Apr 2026)

Project scope management ensures that a project includes only the work required to complete the project successfully — and nothing extra. It defines what is included and what is NOT included in a project.

Scope Creep = uncontrolled expansion of project scope → NOT allowed. Without scope management: projects may grow uncontrollably; cost and time increase unexpectedly; team may lose focus on objectives.

6 Main Processes in Project Scope Management

1
Plan Scope Management: Defines how scope will be defined, validated, and controlled.
2
Collect Requirements: Gathers expectations from stakeholders.
3
Define Scope: Converts requirements into a clear project scope statement. Tools: Interview, Survey, Workshop.
4
Create WBS (Work Breakdown Structure): Breaks project into smaller manageable parts.
5
Validate Scope: Formal acceptance of completed deliverables by stakeholders.
6
Control Scope: Monitors changes during the project. Prevents uncontrolled expansion. Any change must go through change control system.

Project Risk Management (Class 8 — 30 Apr 2026)

A risk is any uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, can have positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) impact on a project.
Change is the father of risk.
Positive Risk → Creates opportunity (e.g., technology advancing faster than expected)
Negative Risk → Creates threat (e.g., key team member leaving the project)

4 Processes of Risk Management

1
Risk Identification: Determining risks that are likely to affect a project.
2
Risk Quantification: Evaluating risks to assess possible project outcomes. Methods: EVA (Expected Monetary Value analysis), PERT estimation, Simulation, Expert judgement, Delphi method.
3
Risk Response Development: Taking steps to enhance opportunities and develop responses to each threat.
• Risk Avoidance: Eliminate the cause of the risk
• Risk Acceptance: Accept the consequences if risk occurs
• Risk Mitigation: Reduce the impact or probability of the risk
4
Risk Response Control: Responding to risks over the course of the project. Uses a Risk Register. Includes: risk monitoring, risk audit, variance & trend analysis, reserve analysis, technical performance measurement, meetings & reviews.

3 Types of Project Risk

TypeDescription
Market RiskAssociated with determining if the new product will be useful to the organization or marketable — will users accept or use the product/service?
Financial RiskAssociated with determining if the organization can afford to undertake the project and if the project is the best way to use company's financial resources.
Technology RiskAssociated with determining if the project is technically feasible and if technology can become obsolete before the useful product can be produced.
Residual Risk: Risk that remains after all risk responses have been implemented.
Risk Register Contains: Identified risks, impact, response plan, current status, probability.

Project Procurement Management (Class 9 — 22 Apr 2026)

Procurement generally means acquiring goods or services from an outside source.

4 Core Knowledge Areas: Scope, Time, Cost, Quality
4 Facilitating Knowledge Areas: Procurement, Human Resource, Communication, Risk

Causes / Reasons for Procurement

1. To provide flexibility for the organization to focus on core business.
2. To gain access to technical skills, expertise, and technologies that the organization doesn't possess.

6 Procurement Management Processes

ProcessDescriptionPhase
1. Procurement PlanningIdentifying which project needs can best be met using products/services outside the organization. Make-or-buy analysis.Planning
2. Solicitation PlanningDocumenting product requirements and identifying potential vendors (material, parts, components).Planning
3. SolicitationObtaining quotations, bids, offers, or proposals from vendors. Request for Quotation (RFQ).Executing
4. Source SelectionSelecting the best offer for the best product from vendors.Executing
5. Contract AdministrationManaging the contractual relationship with the vendor.Controlling
6. Contract CloseoutFormal closing of the contract. Verifies all work was completed correctly and satisfactorily.Closing
Project Phases: Initializing → Planning → Executing → Controlling → Closing

Make-or-Buy Analysis

A process to determine whether a particular product or service should be made inside the organization or purchased from outside. Considers: What to make/buy, How much, When (timing).
